Did the trend hold up in later games?

To check a trend later, freeze what you knew when you spotted it. Find past points in time with a similar rate, then count what happened in the following games. Keep those later games out of the spot choice. Show the pooled count and its range, and withhold a score when there are too few games.

A test that starts in the past

A recent rate describes the games that made it. A later-games study asks another question: after a similar past rate, what followed? I keep those questions separate. If later results help choose an earlier spot, the study has already seen its answer. That can make a tidy table without making a fair test.

Coming BookBreaker will show how often similar past trends reached the number in later games, with a count and a Wilson range.

The planned method

A trend uses one player or team, one stat, a threshold you type, and the last 5, 10 or 20 games played, or the season. It may be split by home, away or opponent.

The five rate bands are under 40%, 40 to 59%, 60 to 69%, 70 to 79%, and 80% and over.

Walk through games in date order across this season and the previous one. After each game, use the window ending at that game. A rate in the current band makes a spot. Games after the spot cannot choose it.

After a spot, count threshold reaches in the next 5 games the subject played. The next spot for that band cannot start until those games have passed. Within that calculation, each later game counts once.

Pool the later games as x of y, with a Wilson 95% range. The end of a season may leave fewer than 5 games after a spot. Under 15 later games, show “too few past spots to tell” and no score.

The score counts threshold reaches in later games. It does not ask whether a later rate stayed inside the earlier band. Those are different outcomes. The band chooses the past situations to inspect; the following games supply the outcome.

One season of team scores

For this worked study I use all 32 NFL teams in the 2025 regular season from the local schedules file. The threshold is 24 or more team points. The window is the last 10 played games, ending at each spot. The horizon is the next 5 played games. Postseason games are excluded. This is one season of team scores, with no player data.

Coming Player game logs for every league will come with BookBreaker. The worked examples here use team scores; the NFL player study uses public weekly stats.

This study is narrower than the planned method’s two-season search. No earlier season is joined to this calculation. A team needs a full window before its first eligible spot. I run each band as a separate question for each team. Once a spot is selected, I skip ahead until its horizon has passed before selecting another spot for that same band.

Near the end, every available later game counts, even if the full horizon is not available. I pool reaches and games across teams, rather than taking an average of team percentages.

No future game repeats within a team’s calculation for one band. A game can appear in more than one row because the bands are studied independently. Do not add the rows together as though they were a fresh set of distinct games. Teams can also play one another, so these results are not fully independent trials.

What this study can support

Read the counts before comparing the rounded rates. A higher starting band does not require a higher later rate. The table reports what this file gives under these rules. I have not searched thresholds to find a pleasing order. The Wilson ranges are the same binomial calculation used elsewhere on this site, with the same limits when games are related.

This is descriptive evidence from a fixed season, not a forecast check on a new season. Team points cannot establish how player points behave. A useful next check would hold the rules fixed and use another period. It would still need its own source rows and counts.

Count games the subject played. Skip missed games in both the window and the later games. Player minutes and snaps are outside the first version.

When a provider corrects a stat, the next nightly file will carry it. Rates and scores will be worked out again from the stored logs. No result will be kept across nights.

The number for a kind of trend pools subjects within a league, for the same stat family, window and band. It has its own counts.

Questions

Can changing a later score create an earlier spot?

It must not. A spot uses the window ending at that game. Tests change every later score and check that the earlier selection stays the same. The later outcome may change, which is expected.

What happens with fewer than 15 later games?

The page withholds the rate and range and says “too few past spots to tell.” The later-game count remains visible. A zero count is not a score of zero.

Is this the app running?

No. This table is built from the local team-scores file by the website generator. The app is still in development. The method and the source are stated so this page can be checked before a desktop build exists.
