Deposit match calculator

By Matt Kerr · Updated 4 November 2026

A deposit match is worth what is left after the rollover. Every dollar you must bet to release the bonus costs the book's margin, so a large match with a heavy rollover can be worth less than a small one. This page works out what you keep.

Check your own deposit match

Enter the deposit, the bonus, the playthrough multiple and one book's prices for every side of the cheapest market the terms let you bet. You get the hold the offer breaks even at and what it is worth on average.

A deposit match is a loan of turnover, not a gift, and the interest is the hold on whatever markets the terms let you churn through.

The offer: deposit $1000, receive $1000 in bonus funds, clear 10x rollover before anything can be withdrawn.

Break-even hold is the number that decides it. It sits at 5.0%. Churn below that and the bonus survives to the cashier. Churn above it and the bonus was spent before the terms released it.

So the clause that matters is the one listing eligible markets. Both holds below are worked from typed example markets (a main line and a player prop), not measured at any book.

Same headline, same deposit, same rollover, and a swing of $1572.11 between them. The market restriction is the offer. The bonus figure is packaging.

One number to two decimals reads as if it were measured, and the eligible-markets line is what decides it. Where terms push churn into parlays the cost climbs again; our parlay hold prior of 15.0% is a prior, not a measurement, and it is deliberately excluded from the table above.

The arithmetic behind both rows

In this offer the rollover is counted on the deposit and the bonus together: 10 times $2,000 is $20,000 that has to be bet before a withdrawal.

Every dollar through costs the hold of the market it goes through. On moneylines at 4.55% that is $909.09 of expected cost against a $1,000 bonus, leaving $90.91. On props at 12.41% it is $2,481.20, more than the bonus, a loss of $1,481.20.

Break-even is the bonus over the turnover: $1,000 over $20,000 is 5.0%. Any market holding less than that leaves the bonus worth something.

When to work a deposit match through

Before you deposit, not after. The terms that decide it are the rollover multiple, what it is counted on, and which markets count towards it.

When choosing between two offers. A smaller match with an easy rollover can be worth more than a big one that forces you through props or parlays.

When you can bet both sides across books. A low-hold pair churns the rollover for less than any single book's hold.
