Totals middle calculator

How do you price a totals middle bet?

A middle is a pair of bets on one game: the over at the lower line, the under at the higher one. Both win when the final total lands inside the window between them. Outside it, one side pays, the other loses, and the miss costs the vig.

The lines priced here are 44.5 and 47.5, a window 3 points wide. Break-even is 4.76% — the rate the window must hit for the pair to be worth holding. That is arithmetic on both prices and nothing else: no distribution, no sample, no view on the sport. It is the honest anchor.

The window probability is 9.19%. That one is a normal approximation, and its spread of 13.0 points is a stated prior, not a measurement. Competitors print a figure like it out to the decimal as though it had been observed.

Counting beats a smooth curve. Real scores cluster on the totals a sport actually produces. A curve cannot reproduce a lump, so it moves mass into gaps where finals never land. Count finals, and hold the sample to a threshold first.

Totals carry the higher bar: wider support, more distinct finals, each seen less often. Below any of these minimums the counted estimate is noise wearing a decimal point.
