What the price read says

The cheapest yearly price in these saved reads was $59.99, Linemate’s Annual in-app price.

A monthly listing and an annual listing answer different billing questions. Check the plan name and period together. A lower annual amount is not proof that it includes everything in a higher listing. The saved notes record the price range without a full feature map for each in-app purchase. I would check that map before comparing a specific plan.

The worked-cost page keeps the lower and higher annual amounts as separate rows. It also separates the planned app licence from later data updates. That makes the time period visible. It does not assume that either product’s data or features are the same. A comparison is easier to use when its gaps are left in view.

What an existing rating history adds

Linemate had a 4.84 App Store average from 14,347 ratings on the saved read.

That is evidence from people who have already rated an available app. It is a real head start over an app still in development. A rating is also a different kind of evidence from a checked answer to a stats question. I would use the rating to decide what to inspect, then check the workflow that matters to me.

The average belongs with its read date and rating count. Do not copy it as though it were measured today. The saved notes do not establish that every rating concerned the same feature. They also do not replace trying the particular table or filter you need. A useful comparison leaves room for both kinds of check.

The later-games idea is a separate test of past situations. It needs rules for which spots qualify, how many later games count, and when to withhold a score. I explain those rules with a worked team-points study. That study is evidence for the website’s calculation, rather than proof that a finished app has passed a player-log test.

To compare a future card, I would choose a player and a threshold, write the window down, and count the source rows. Then I would compare the game dates included by each view. That gives any disagreement somewhere concrete to start. An attractive percentage with no trace back to its rows is harder to check.

Questions

Can I switch to BookBreaker now?

Not yet. BookBreaker is in development. There is no download and no launch date. Use an available source or app for the work you need to do today.

Is Linemate’s annual listing the cheapest read?

The cheapest yearly price in these saved reads was $59.99, Linemate’s Annual in-app price.

Has the saved read checked every Linemate feature?

No. A price and rating read is narrower than a full workflow test. Where the table says a feature was not established, it means the saved notes do not answer that question. It is not a claim that the feature is absent.

Where can I check the counting method first?

Start with the free game-log walkthrough and count one window by hand. Then read the later-games study if you want to see how past spots are chosen without using future results.
