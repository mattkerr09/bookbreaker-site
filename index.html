You do not have to take our word for any of it

Bookbreaker is new and has no customers to quote at you, so this page has no testimonials. What it has instead is a list of claims a stranger can check without trusting us at all — which is the stronger thing to offer about software you are going to place money alongside.

  1. The download matches its published checksum. The build we notarised has SHA-256 735d36ed26e859fa… and the full digest is on the download page. Run shasum -a 256 Bookbreaker-0.1.5.dmg and compare. If it differs, do not open it.
  2. Apple has notarised it, and your Mac will say so. Run spctl -a -t open --context context:primary-signature -vv against the file. It should say accepted and source=Notarized Developer ID. That is Apple confirming the binary, not us.
  3. It makes no network calls. Watch it not make them. There is no updater, no telemetry, no account and no sync — 0 bytes leave your machine, and 0 accounts exist to create. Point Little Snitch or lsof -i -p $(pgrep Bookbreaker) at it and watch nothing happen. A test in the repo fails the build if a network library is ever linked in.
  4. Every figure on this site was computed by the app you download. Not typed. The build runs the same engine — 37 modules, 40 test files — and refuses to publish a number that engine did not produce. That is why the fill figure here and the one in a fresh install differ, and why the page says which is which.
  5. Every legal claim carries its source and the date we read it. 29 regulator and statute links across the state pages, each with a read date, each checked by the build. A dead citation fails the build rather than sitting there looking official.

What it will never do, including the profitable things

A tool for money is defined as much by its refusals. These are ours, and they are enforced in code rather than promised in a policy page.

Never asks for a sportsbook login.Competitors sync your accounts with your credentials. We import a CSV or read a pasted betslip. Same ledger, and your passwords stay yours.
Never touches identity, KYC, device or location.The account-longevity model reads stake, timing, market and velocity — bet shape, nothing else. It has no access to who you are and never will.
Never places a bet for you.Automated placement needs your logins and has to look human to the book. It is the fastest known route to being limited, which is the opposite of the point.
Never publishes a number it did not measure.Where a figure is a stated prior rather than something observed, the page says so in the same sentence. The build fails if it does not.

From a price on screen to a decision

The bet you place late is a different bet

A quote you take 15.4s after it was posted is worth 3.37% of a 4% edge, not 4%. Everything in the window is built around getting you to an answer before that decay eats it.

  1. Open it

    No account, no login, no sync. It runs offline, so there is no round trip between you and an answer.

  2. Type the price

    Every field is already filled with a worked example, so you can see the shape of the answer before you have typed anything. Replace the numbers that differ.

  3. Press Enter

    Enter runs the tab you are in. Reaching for the mouse to submit a four-character form is the slowest part of a fast decision.

  4. Check it is still there

    The fifth tab prices the quote's age against the book's own latency and tells you the chance it fills — 84% on a 10s quote, against the 89% a screen that ignores its own lag would show you.

And the account still has to be there tomorrow

An edge you get limited out of is a hobby

Risk desks do not read your intentions. They read the shape of your betting: how precise the stakes are, which markets you pick, how fast you react. Bookbreaker scores that shape and spends a named amount of edge to soften it.

What it will never do: no multi-accounting, no identity or KYC workarounds, no device or location spoofing. The model reads bet attributes only — stake, timing, market, velocity — and has no access to identity or network state. That line is drawn in the code, not in a policy page.
