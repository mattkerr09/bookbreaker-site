Can I pay once for a sports stats app?

Separate the app licence from the cost of fresh games. Paying once can mean keeping the software, while new data still has a yearly fee. Compare both totals over the time you expect to use it. Also check what you can still open if you stop paying for updates, before choosing a plan.

Keep the licence and the updates separate

Coming BookBreaker will cost $39 once, with 12 months of new games included. After that, new games will be an optional $19 a year.

Coming Without the yearly update, BookBreaker will keep every stat and tool it already has. New games will stop arriving.

The word “once” needs a clear object. It can refer to the app licence without covering every future season’s data. I would write down both parts before comparing bills. One question is what you can keep opening. The other is what you pay to keep receiving fresh games. A plan should answer both in plain words.

The cheapest yearly price in these saved reads was $59.99, Linemate’s Annual in-app price.

The source amounts

Linemate in-app prices read: monthly $9.99 to $19.99; annual $59.99 to $79.99.

PropsMadness listed a $0 free tier and Pro at $25 a month or $250 billed yearly.

Dimers Pro listed a sale annual price of $99.99 for the first year, then $199.99 a year. It also listed 2 months at $14.99 a month, then $29.99 a month, and 6 months for $149.99.

For the planned desktop rows, the first year includes the licence and its included new games. Each additional year of new games adds the planned update price. The row without later updates holds the licence cost fixed. That row cannot be read as a plan for fresh games every season. The difference matters when you choose the time period for your comparison.

Ask what remains on your computer

Coming BookBreaker will need no account and will keep your history on your computer.

Coming BookBreaker will work offline with the last stats file it has.

A low total is useful only if it describes the work you want to do. Before choosing any app, ask what happens to old results when a paid period ends. Ask whether a log needs another sign-in to open. If the saved source does not answer, check that point directly rather than filling the gap with an assumption.

I want the planned price to be easy to understand before there is a purchase button. BookBreaker is in development. There is no download or checkout here. These tables make the proposed payment split visible while the app is still being built. They cannot establish which available plan fits a particular reader’s needs.

Why not multiply the monthly price by 12?

That would answer the cost of paying monthly at that price. It would not prove that the rival sells a yearly plan at the same total. This table uses stated annual amounts and leaves unread plans blank.

Does the lowest price decide which app to use?

No. Check the features, device and data you need as well as the bill. Two plans can have different jobs even when their annual totals are close. Read the plan details before choosing.
