What “no account” means

An account is a record about you kept on the maker’s server. It usually holds an email address, a password and the settings tied to them. A stats app with no account skips that record. You open it and read numbers without signing up first. That is a narrower promise than “never goes online,” because new games still have to arrive from somewhere.

History is the other half of the question. Saved views, the players you follow and the numbers you typed can live in a sync service or in a file on your disk. With no account there is nowhere to sync them, so they stay on the computer that made them. The trade is that backups become your job.

Coming BookBreaker will need no account and will keep your history on your computer.

A download is still a contact with a server. That server sees an internet address and the time of the request, as any web page does. So “no account” and “sends nothing” are separate claims, and each one deserves its own question. Besides new games, a licence check is the usual reason a paid local app makes contact.

Three checks you can run on any app

Checking a no-account claim yourself
CheckHowWhat it tells you
Sign-in screenInstall the app and open it freshWhether a sign-up step comes before any numbers
Privacy pageSearch it for account, email, sync, cloud and third partiesWhat the maker says it collects
Offline testTurn off Wi-Fi, then open a saved viewWhether your history and data are local

The offline test is the strongest of the three. If a saved view opens with the network off, the data and your history are on the machine. To see the files, look in the AppData folder inside your user folder on Windows, or in Library, Application Support, in your home folder on a Mac. Copy that folder before you reinstall or move to a new computer. With no account, nothing restores it for you.

The planned approach

Coming Without the yearly update, BookBreaker will keep every stat and tool it already has. New games will stop arriving.

Coming BookBreaker will cost $39 once, with 12 months of new games included. After that, new games will be an optional $19 a year.

A one-time licence fits a no-account design, because a key can be checked without a profile behind it. When the privacy details appear, the licence check is the part to read closely.
